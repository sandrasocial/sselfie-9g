A Note Before You Start
I used to post three times a week and then disappear for three weeks.

Not because I didn't care. I cared so much it kept me up at night. But every Sunday I'd sit down to plan my content and just... stare. Nothing. A blank page and a growing sense that I was falling behind.

I'd eventually write something in a panic on a Tuesday. It never felt right. And then I'd go quiet again and feel terrible about it.

What I didn't realise was that the problem wasn't discipline. It was the absence of a plan. I wasn't lazy — I just had no system.

This changes that. By Sunday, you have your whole week ready. Not just ideas — actual caption starters, your theme, your content types, all of it. Done before Monday morning.

Thirty days planned. One Sunday at a time.

— Sandra

Exercise One

Your Month
In 4 Themes

Each week has one theme. A theme is the one thing your audience needs to hear from you that week — the thread that ties all your content together. When you have a theme, everything you post feels intentional instead of random.

You don't have to be wildly creative here. Think about what's real in your business right now. What's happening? What are you launching, thinking about, working through? Your life is the source material.

Question 01 — What Are The 4 Things You Talk About Most?

Don't overthink it. If someone asked your clients what you're known for, what would they say? List the topics, skills, or ideas that come up again and again in your work.

Question 02 — What's Happening In Your Business This Month?

A launch, a new offer, a milestone, a shift in thinking — anything that's live and real. Your content should connect to where your business actually is right now.

Question 03 — What Do You Want People To DO After 30 Days?

Not just what you want them to feel — what's the action? Follow you? Book a call? Buy your offer? Sign up for something? Know the destination before you start.

Exercise Two

The Daily 5
Formula

Here's the thing nobody tells you: you don't need 30 different ideas. You need 5 content types and a rotation. That's it. You run these 5 types on repeat and your feed stays varied, interesting, and purposeful — every single month.

The 5 Content Types

Story · Strategy · Social Proof · Show Up · Sell

S
Story — Personal

A real moment from your life or business. Vulnerable. Honest. The kind of post that makes someone think "that's exactly how I feel." This is where people fall in love with you.

Str
Strategy — Tip or Lesson

Something you know that helps your audience. A quick win they can use today. Not showing off — genuinely teaching. This is where they trust you.

SP
Social Proof — Result or Testimonial

A win from a client or student. A result you've seen. What becomes possible when someone works with you or uses your method. This is where they believe you.

SU
Show Up — Behind the Scenes

A peek into your real process. Your workspace, your Sunday planning session, your drafts, your morning. This builds intimacy. It makes you human and real.

Se
Sell — Offer Mention

Your product, service, or offer. Clear, direct, no apology. People can't buy from you if they don't know what you sell. You're allowed to mention it.

Which Content Type Feels Most Natural To You?

The one you actually enjoy writing. The one that comes out without effort. That's probably where your voice is strongest — lean into it first.

Which Content Type Do You Avoid — And Why?

Be honest. Sell? Strategy? Knowing where you avoid helps you plan ahead so you're not skipping it every week.

Exercise Four

Your Sunday
Reset

This is the ritual that makes everything else work. Thirty minutes every Sunday. That's it. Not every morning. Not every day. Once a week, you sit down with this plan and your whole week is sorted before Monday starts.

You've done the hard planning work already. Now you're just filling in the week. It gets faster every time.

1
Pick Your Theme For The Week

Go back to your 4-Week Theme Planner from Exercise 01. Which week is this? What's the one thing your audience needs to hear from you right now? Write it at the top of a page.

2
Choose Your 5 Content Types

You're posting 5 times this week. Decide which day gets which type — Story, Strategy, Social Proof, Show Up, Sell. Use the calendar as your guide. No stress about the order, just pick what makes sense.

3
Fill In 5 Caption Starters

Go to Exercise 03 and pick one starter per post. Copy it, paste it into your notes or your scheduler, and write two to three sentences that finish the thought in your own voice. Done.

4
Schedule or Batch-Write

Either schedule all 5 posts in your app of choice, or write them now and save them as drafts. Either way, the week is ready. You're not writing under pressure on a Tuesday morning.

5
Done — Close The Laptop

That's the whole ritual. Thirty minutes. Whole week sorted. Put it away and go do something you enjoy. You've already shown up for the week.

What Time Sunday Works For You?

Be specific. "Sunday evening" is less real than "Sunday at 8pm after the kids are in bed." When exactly will this happen? Put it in your calendar right now.

What Would Make This Feel Easier?

What's the version of this ritual that you'd actually do? Less steps? A playlist? A specific spot? Remove any friction between you and this habit.

How Many Posts Can You Realistically Create Each Week?

Be honest. Three posts you actually publish beats seven posts you abandon. Pick the number your real life can hold.

Which Formats Fit You Best Right Now?

Story, Reel, carousel, static post, email, voice note. Choose the formats you can repeat without burning out.

What Content Assets Do You Already Have?

Selfies, testimonials, screenshots, old posts, notes, client questions, behind-the-scenes clips. Your plan gets easier when you start with what already exists.

What Can You Repurpose This Week?

One strong idea can become a Story, post, email, and Reel. Pick one thing you can reuse instead of creating from scratch.
