# 1. The Core Founder Story

Hey, I'm Sandra.

I'm a mum of three, born and raised in Iceland, and now I live in Norway.

And for a long time, I felt like I was waiting for someone else to tell me what to do with my life.

Not just in business.

In everything.

I was overwhelmed.
I was impulsive.
I had a head full of ideas and no clear direction.
I could see other women online building something, showing up, creating content, making it look so easy.

And honestly?

I kept thinking, why not me?

But then I would overthink everything.

What do I post?
What do I say?
Who am I to do this?
What if people judge me?
What if I start and fail?

So I stayed in that weird place between wanting more and not trusting myself enough to move.

Then life changed.

After a 14-year relationship, we separated.

And I want to say this clearly: that is not the whole story.

It was a chapter.
A hard one.
A scary one.
A chapter that forced me to look at my life differently.

But this story is not about the separation.

This story is about what happened after.

I moved into a two-bedroom apartment with my three kids and our dog. The boys shared one room. My daughter had the other. I slept on the couch.

It was not aesthetic.
It was not planned.
It was not some beautiful fresh-start movie scene.

It was an empty fridge.
A tight bank account.
A tired mum trying to act brave.
A woman standing in a tiny apartment thinking, okay... what now?

And weirdly, inside all that fear, I started to feel something else.

A tiny bit of space.
A tiny bit of clarity.
A tiny bit of me coming back.

For years, I had believed things like:

I need someone else to provide for me.
I'm too old to start over.
I'm too messy to be taken seriously.
My ADHD brain is too much.
I don't know enough.
I don't have enough.
I'm not ready.

But when everything felt stripped back, I had to ask myself a better question.

What if I already have enough to start?

Not enough to have it all figured out.

Just enough for the first step.

And my first step was my phone.

That was it.

No fancy studio.
No photographer.
No team.
No big plan.

Just my phone, my selfies, my story, and the decision to stop hiding.

I started creating again.

At first, it was messy.
The lighting was bad.
The angles were weird.
My apartment did not have the big beautiful windows I used to have.
I tried filming in every corner and nothing looked right.

So I got scrappy.

I bought cheap softboxes.
I used a ring light.
I put my phone on a cheap tripod.
I turned my bathroom into a little studio.

And honestly, that bathroom studio taught me more than any perfect setup ever could.

It taught me that creativity does not wait for perfect conditions.
It taught me that your phone is enough to begin.
It taught me that confidence is not something you find before you show up.

You build it by showing up.

One selfie.
One post.
One messy video.
One small brave moment at a time.

That is where SSELFIE started.

Not as an AI brand.
Not as a perfect business.
Not because I had everything together.

It started because I knew what it felt like to feel invisible.

And I knew other women felt it too.

The woman who hates photos of herself.
The woman who opens Instagram and freezes.
The woman who wants to build something, but has no idea what to say.
The woman who is curious about AI, but does not want to look fake.
The woman who wants her own money, her own thing, her own creative world.
The woman who keeps saving ideas but never starts.

I built SSELFIE for her.

Because I genuinely believe a woman does not need a perfect life, perfect body, perfect home, or perfect plan to become visible.

She can start with what she already has.

Her phone.
Her face.
Her story.
Her everyday life.
Her taste.
Her ideas.
Her voice.

And yes, AI can help her create faster.
It can help her make beautiful brand photos.
It can help her plan, write, design, and stop staring at a blank screen.

But AI is not the point.

She is the point.

The selfie gets attention.
The story builds connection.
The message builds trust.
The offer creates income.

That is what I teach now.

And if there is one thing I want every woman to understand, it is this:

You do not become ready by waiting.

You become ready by starting.
