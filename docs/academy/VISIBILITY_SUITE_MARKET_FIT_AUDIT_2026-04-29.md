# Visibility Suite Market Fit Audit

Date: 2026-04-29  
Products: What To Say, Show Up, Get Paid, Visibility Suite bundle  
Owner: Sandra / SSELFIE  
Status: Working plan

---

## 1. Why This Audit Exists

The Visibility Suite should not become three pretty workbooks that leave the user thinking:

> I filled this out. Now what?

The suite needs to solve a real buyer problem:

> I want to build a visible personal brand, but I do not know what to say, what to post, or how to turn attention into money.

This audit checks whether the current products are:

1. Something the target customer actively needs.
2. Something she understands quickly.
3. Something she would pay for.
4. Something that creates a clear next step after the workbook is complete.

---

## 2. Market Signal Summary

Current creator and personal-brand markets support this suite direction.

Observed market signals:

- Creators are moving toward direct monetization: digital products, paid communities, memberships, coaching, templates, and service offers.
- Buyers are overwhelmed by content advice. They want simple systems and clear action.
- Static education is weaker when it does not produce an immediate output.
- Higher-value offers are easier to justify when the buyer receives implementation help, feedback, or a personalized plan.
- Personal brand clarity, audience trust, and repeatable visibility are still strong demand areas.

References used:

- Kajabi creator commerce reporting: https://kajabi.com/state-of-creator-commerce-25
- IAB creator economy report: https://www.iab.com/insights/2025-creator-economy-ad-spend-strategy-report/
- Circle creator economy statistics: https://circle.so/blog/creator-economy-statistics
- Influencer Marketing Factory creator economy report: https://theinfluencermarketingfactory.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Creator-Economy-Report-2026.pdf

Implication for SSELFIE:

The suite has stronger fit when positioned as a visibility-to-income system, not a content workbook bundle.

## 3. Target Buyer

Primary buyer:

- A woman building a personal brand, service, digital product, coaching offer, or small online business.
- She knows she needs to post, but freezes when it is time to write or sell.
- She is likely consuming free advice but still lacks a clear system.
- She wants her business to feel real, visible, and paid.
- She does not want a complex business course.

Core pains:

- "I do not know what to say."
- "I do not know what to post."
- "I post randomly and then disappear."
- "I have something to sell, but I do not know how to talk about it."
- "I do not know if my offer is good enough."
- "I need someone to tell me my next move."

Core buying trigger:

> This would make me look clearer, more consistent, and closer to getting paid.

## 4. Product Fit Verdict

### What To Say

Verdict: Strong fit.

Why it works:

- It solves the first blocker: message confusion.
- The questions help the user define the person, problem, transformation, story, voice, and content buckets.
- This is the natural entry point for someone who feels frozen.

Risk:

- It can sound like "caption help" if positioned too narrowly.
- Several questions overlap around identity, story, expertise, values, vision, and voice.

Best positioning:

> Know what to say so people understand why they should follow, trust, and buy from you.

Required outcome:

- One-line positioning statement.
- Audience snapshot.
- Brand voice words.
- Four content buckets.
- First 5-10 post prompts.

### Show Up

Verdict: Strong fit if it is framed as a consistency system.

Why it works:

- The buyer wants consistency but does not want a complicated content calendar.
- The Sunday ritual and five content types are easy to understand.
- It turns "I should post" into a weekly rhythm.

Risk:

- "Show up" can sound motivational instead of practical unless the output is obvious.
- It should not feel like another blank calendar.

Best positioning:

> Know what to post this week without starting from zero every morning.

Required outcome:

- Four-week theme map.
- Weekly content rhythm.
- Five content types.
- Sunday planning ritual.
- First 7 days of post ideas.

### Get Paid

Verdict: Highest-value product in the suite.

Why it works:

- It connects visibility to revenue.
- It answers the buyer's deeper fear: "Will this actually make money?"
- It can naturally upsell into private coaching.

Risk:

- It must avoid income guarantees.
- It needs to help the user choose one realistic offer, not build a complex business model.

Best positioning:

> Turn your visibility into a simple paid offer people can say yes to.

Required outcome:

- Clear offer statement.
- Buyer sentence.
- Starter price or price range.
- Sales post.
- DM or email script.
- First revenue action.

### Visibility Suite Bundle

Verdict: Strong bundle fit.

Why it works:

- The three products form a natural sequence:
  1. What To Say = message.
  2. Show Up = rhythm.
  3. Get Paid = revenue.
- This is easier to sell as a complete path than as disconnected workbooks.

Risk:

- If the Academy hub shows the products as separate random cards, the user will not understand the sequence.
- The suite needs a buyer home that tells her where to start and what happens after each workbook.

Best positioning:

> The simple visibility system for women who want to know what to say, what to post, and how to turn attention into income.

Required outcome:

- One suite home.
- Three product cards in order.
- A clear "start here" path.
- One final Maya-generated plan.
- Upsell bridge into 4-week private sprint.

- Keep: strong and directly tied to a user outcome.
- Rewrite: valuable, but wording or placement should be clearer.
- Combine: overlaps with another question.
- Remove: does not produce a useful decision or output.
- Maya Output: answer should feed a generated asset.
- Upsell Bridge: answer reveals where private help is valuable.

## 6. Suite Positioning Recommendation

Current suite should be framed as:

> Your visibility-to-paid system.

Supporting copy:

> Start with your message. Build a rhythm you can keep. Turn it into a simple offer people can say yes to.

Avoid:

- "Workbook bundle"
- "Content templates"
- "Marketing system"
- "AI-powered business transformation"

Use:

- "What to say"
- "What to post"
- "What to sell"
- "Your next step"
- "Your first paid path"

## 9. Metrics To Watch

Product-market fit indicators:

- Product page view to checkout click rate.
- Checkout start to purchase rate.
- Workbook open rate after purchase.
- Workbook completion rate.
- Maya plan generation rate.
- Return visits to suite home.
- Clicks to private sprint.
- Refunds or support questions.

Qualitative signals:

- Users say "this helped me know what to do."
- Users ask for feedback on their offer or content.
- Users share finished outputs.
- Users ask for 1:1 help after seeing their gaps.

Red flags:

- Users buy but do not open the workbooks.
- Users open the workbooks but do not fill them in.
- Users fill in answers but do not generate or use outputs.
- Users ask what the difference is between the three products.
- Users think the suite is only caption templates.
