I had 3,000 followers. Nice photos. Comments. Saves. DMs that said "I love your content."

01
Session One

What You're
Actually Selling

Most women are "selling" their general vibe. Their energy. Their presence. That is hard to buy. People buy a clear next step. A specific one, with a believable before and after, in a timeframe they can understand.

Before you write a sales post, before you set a price, before you DM anyone — you need to know exactly what you're offering. Not approximately. Exactly.

Question 01 — What Exact Result Do You Help Someone Achieve?

Not "I help women feel confident." What does that actually look like at the end? What can they do, say, earn, or feel that they couldn't before? Be specific.

If you are stuck, start with: I can help someone with [specific problem]. I can create [simple offer]. I can invite [type of person].

Question 02 — How Long Does It Take?

Days? Weeks? One session? A month? Vague timelines kill trust. The more specific you are, the more believable it is.

Question 03 — What Does Their Life Look Like Before vs. After?

Before they work with you: what are they struggling with, avoiding, feeling stuck on? After: what's different? What changed?

Your Offer Sentence

Read it back. Does it sound like something real that a real person would pay for? If not — it's not the words that are wrong. It's that the offer isn't specific enough yet. Keep going.

Offer Readiness Check

Before you sell, make sure your offer is clear enough.

Can someone understand what it is in one sentence?
Do they know who it is for?
Do they know what result it helps them move toward?
Do they know what they get?
Do they know how to buy?
Do they know what is not included?

If any of these feel unclear, fix the offer before you write the sales post.

02
Session Two

Who
Pays

Your buyer is not your follower. They're not even necessarily your most loyal fan. A buyer is a very specific kind of person: someone with a problem that hurts, interest in solving it, and enough trust in you to take the next step.

Those three things have to be true at the same time. Most of your followers only have one. A buyer has all three.

Buyer Signals

Look for people who say things like:

  • "I need this."
  • "I don't know where to start."
  • "How do I do this?"
  • "Can you send me the link?"
  • "I wish I had your confidence."
  • "I've been thinking about this for a while."
  • "How much is it?"
  • "Do you offer help with this?"

These are not just compliments. They are signals.

Question 01 — Who Has Already Paid You?

Even €50. Even a coffee meeting. Even a favour that felt like a transaction. Who are the people who have already said "yes" to you in some form? Write them down.

Question 02 — What Were They Struggling With?

Not what they said they wanted — what was actually bothering them when they came to you? The real problem under the surface.

Question 03 — What Did They Say After?

The exact words. What changed for them? What did they tell you when it worked? This is your testimonial. This is your sales copy. This is your proof.

Session Three

The €500
Path

A €500 path is not a guarantee. It is a way to make the math visible so you can choose one clear offer, one clear price, and one clear way to invite people.

1 One client

One person pays €500. One session, one service, one offer at that price.

5 Five clients

Five people each pay you €100. A workshop, a mini-course, a short consult.

10 Ten clients

Ten people each pay you €50. A guide, a template, a simple digital product.

Question 01 — Which Path Feels Most Realistic Right Now?

Not the most exciting. Not the most impressive. The most honest answer — given where you are today, your audience, and your energy.

Question 02 — What Would You Need To Sell?

If you chose that path — what is the actual thing? A call? A file? A session? A service? Be exact. Give it a name.

Question 03 — What's Stopping You From Offering It Today?

Be honest. Not the official reason — the real one. Is it that it's not ready? Or is it that you're scared of what happens if you say it out loud?

Session Four

Your
Sales Post

A sales post is not a pitch. Nobody wants to be pitched. A sales post is a story that ends with an invitation. You're not selling to them — you're recognising them and offering them a way through.

There's a structure that works. Five parts. Write one part at a time. Don't edit as you go. Just write.

Part 01 — The Hook

Start with the problem they recognise in themselves. Not "are you struggling with..." — that's a pitch. A hook is a statement they see themselves in.

Part 02 — Your Story

Share your experience with this same problem. Not how you solved it — just the moment you felt it. Real, specific, honest.

Part 03 — The Bridge

What changed? Not a miracle. A specific thing you did or understood that shifted it.

Part 04 — The Offer

What you have for them. The name, what it does, who it's for. One paragraph. Clear and honest.

Part 05 — The CTA

One clear next step. Not three options. One thing they can do right now to move forward.

10 Hook Starters — Use Any That Feels True
  • "You've been posting for months. You have followers. You have engagement. But nobody knows what the next step is."
  • "The problem isn't that you're not visible enough. The problem is you have nothing clear to buy."
  • "I used to think I needed a bigger audience before I could make an offer. That belief kept me hiding the next step."
  • "Here's something nobody tells you: getting likes and getting paid are two completely different skills."
  • "If someone looked at your Instagram right now — would they know what to buy from you? Or would they just know you're nice?"
  • "The gap between showing up and getting paid is not more content. It's one clear offer."
  • "Most women I know undercharge, underprice, or don't price at all. And then they wonder why they're exhausted."
  • "You've done the hard part. You're visible. Now let's make the next step clear."
  • "I kept posting without selling. Not because nobody saw me. Because nobody knew what to ask me for."
  • "Your followers can love your content and still not know how to work with you. A clear offer changes that."
05
Session Five

Ask For
The Sale

Most women never ask. They hint. They hope. They "let people know" something exists and then wait to see if anyone bites. That's not sales. That's wishing.

A direct ask feels awkward until you realise that it's actually respectful. You're not manipulating anyone. You're saying clearly: I have something that helps. Do you want it? That's honest. That's kind.

The awkwardness is yours to manage. It's not a reason to stay silent.

Something Real
The first time I asked directly — not hinted, not "mentioned" — I felt like I wanted to crawl out of my skin. I DM'd a woman I'd spoken to three times and I just said: "I think this would really help you. Want me to send you the details?"

She said yes within four minutes.

The only thing that had changed was that I asked. That's it. Same offer. Same person. Same conversation. Just the ask.

Now it's your turn. Write three ways you could directly ask someone to work with you this week. Not hint. Not suggest. Ask.

Script 01 — The DM

A direct message to someone you've spoken to before. Short. Warm. Specific about why you're reaching out to them.

Script 02 — The Caption

A post that ends with a direct, specific invite. Not "link in bio if interested." An actual ask.

Script 03 — The Email or Voice Note

For someone on your email list, or a friend you've been meaning to reach out to. A voice note also works — sometimes better.

Buyer Urgency — Why Would She Want This Now?

What makes this problem feel current? A launch, a new season, bills, burnout, a goal, a deadline, or the frustration of staying stuck too long.

Willingness To Pay — Has She Paid For Similar Help Before?

This is your market signal. Has she bought courses, templates, coaching, audits, services, or tools to solve this already?

Delivery Boundary — What Is Included And What Is Not?

Clear boundaries make offers easier to buy and easier to deliver. Write what they get, and what they should not expect.

First 10 Buyers — Who Can You Invite This Week?

List real names, handles, email segments, or groups. Do not wait for the algorithm to find your first buyers.

What Part Of Selling Feels Hardest?

Naming the fear helps you move through it. Is it pricing, being seen, rejection, sounding pushy, or not feeling ready?
