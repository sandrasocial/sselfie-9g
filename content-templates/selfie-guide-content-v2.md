## PART 1: iPhone Settings Cheat Sheet

Before you take another selfie, check these settings. It takes 60 seconds and makes a massive difference.

### Open Your Camera Settings
Go to: **Settings → Camera**

**Turn these ON:**
- ✅ **Grid** — Helps you line up shots and keep your face centered
- ✅ **Mirror Front Camera** — So your selfie looks the way you see yourself (not flipped)
- ✅ **Smart HDR** — Balances light automatically (especially good for harsh lighting)

**Turn these OFF:**
- ❌ **Live Photos** — Uses extra storage and you rarely need the motion
- ❌ **View Outside the Frame** — Distracting while you're shooting

### For iPhone 15/16 Users (2026 Pro Tip):
- Use **Photographic Styles** → Try "Natural" or "Vibrant" for more control over skin tones
- Enable **Action Mode** if you're filming video content (stabilizes shaky hands)
- Use **Portrait Mode** for blurred backgrounds (makes you look more professional)

### Quick Camera Access:
Swipe left from your lock screen. Or double-press the side button (Settings → Accessibility → Side Button).

No more fumbling. Fast selfies = more chances to get the perfect shot.

## PART 2: Lighting Is Everything

Good lighting is 80% of a good selfie. I'm serious.

You can have perfect makeup, perfect hair, perfect outfit... and bad lighting will ruin it.

Here's what actually works.

### Natural Light (Free + Best)

**Golden hour is your best friend.**  
That's 1 hour after sunrise or 1 hour before sunset. Soft, warm, flattering light.

**Face the light source.**  
Window light? Stand facing it. Outside? Face the sun (but not directly — you'll squint).

**Avoid overhead light.**  
Harsh shadows under your eyes = not cute. If you're indoors, move closer to a window.

**Cloudy days are perfect.**  
Clouds act like a giant softbox. Soft, even light. No harsh shadows.

### The Window Technique

This is my go-to indoor setup.

Stand about 3 feet from a large window. Face the window directly. Natural light floods your face evenly. No weird shadows. No harsh lines.

If the light is too bright, put a sheer white curtain over the window. Instant diffusion.

### Artificial Light (When Natural Light Isn't Enough)

**Ring lights.**  
Yes, they work. And they're not just for influencers.

In 2026, there are portable clip-on ring lights that fit in your pocket. Perfect for FaceTime, quick selfies, or filming Stories.

My current favorite setup:
- Small ring light (adjustable color temperature: warm for flattering skin tones, cool for crisp editorial looks)
- Positioned at eye level (not above, not below)
- About 2 feet away from your face

**Pro tip:** If you don't have a ring light, use your laptop screen. Open a blank white page in your browser. Boom — instant soft light source.

### Lighting Mistakes to Avoid:
- ❌ Overhead bathroom lights (create shadows under your eyes and nose)
- ❌ Direct sunlight at noon (too harsh, makes you squint)
- ❌ Backlighting (light behind you = your face is dark)
- ❌ Mixed color temperatures (yellow indoor light + blue window light = weird skin tone)

**The rule:** Light on your face, not behind it.

## PART 3: Angles & Composition

Let's talk angles.

You don't need to be a model. You just need to understand what works for your face.

### The Classic High Angle

**Slightly above eye level.**  
This is the most universally flattering angle. Makes your eyes look bigger, jaw look sharper.

Hold your phone slightly above your head, angle it down about 15-20 degrees. Not so high that you're looking up at the camera (awkward), but high enough that it's not straight-on.

### Find Your Best Side

Everyone has one. I promise.

Take 5 selfies. Turn your head slightly left. Then slightly right. See which side you like better.

For me? Left side. Always. I don't even think about it anymore.

### The "Rule of Thirds"

Remember that grid you turned on in your camera settings?

Position your face in the left or right third of the frame — not dead center. It feels more natural and professional.

Your eyes should sit roughly on the top horizontal line of the grid.

### Eye Contact

Where you look matters.

**Look at the camera lens** (not the screen). This creates real eye contact with whoever sees your photo later.

**Slight angle away** can work too — looking just past the camera creates a more candid, editorial vibe.

**Never look down** at the screen while shooting. It photographs as closed-off and insecure.

### Posture Tricks

**Chin forward and down slightly.**  
This eliminates double chin shadows and defines your jawline. Feels weird. Looks amazing.

**Shoulders back.**  
Good posture reads as confidence.

**Relax your face.**  
Soft smile or neutral expression. Think "I just heard good news" not "CHEESE!"

### Add Depth

Don't stand flat against a wall.

Step away from the background. Creates depth. Makes you stand out.

If you're outside, position yourself so there's something blurred in the background (trees, buildings, bokeh lights at night). Your face becomes the focus.

### Composition Checklist:
- ✅ High angle (phone slightly above eye level)
- ✅ Soft expression (no forced smile — think "content, not cheese")
- ✅ Eyes are in focus (this is where people look first)
- ✅ Background is simple (not cluttered or distracting)
- ✅ You're not dead center (use the rule of thirds)
- ✅ Good posture (shoulders back, chin slightly forward)

## PART 4: What To Wear & How To Style

Your outfit and styling choices show up in selfies more than you think.

### Color Psychology

**Solid colors work best.**  
Patterns can be distracting. Busy prints pull focus from your face.

**Choose colors that complement your skin tone:**
- Warm skin tones: earthy tones, reds, oranges, golds
- Cool skin tones: blues, purples, silvers, jewel tones
- Neutral? You can wear almost anything. Lucky you.

**Black is classic but can wash you out** if you have pale skin or the lighting isn't strong.

**White reflects light beautifully** — great for brightening your face naturally.

### Necklines Matter

**V-necks and scoop necks are universally flattering.**  
They elongate your neck and frame your face.

**Avoid high crew necks in close-up selfies** — they can make your face look wider.

**Off-shoulder or one-shoulder** adds elegance and draws the eye up to your face.

### Hair & Makeup

**Hair:**
- Pull it back or to one side for a clean look
- Avoid covering your face (unless that's the vibe you're going for)
- Natural texture is fine — you don't need a blowout

**Makeup:**
- Slightly more than usual looks natural on camera
- Define your brows (they frame your whole face)
- Add a bit of blush (cameras flatten dimension)
- Matte lips photograph better than super glossy (less glare)

**Pro tip:** Powder any shiny areas (forehead, nose) before shooting. Shine reads as oily on camera.

### Accessories

**Less is more.**
- Earrings: small studs or simple hoops (not giant chandeliers)
- Necklaces: delicate, not chunky (unless that's your brand aesthetic)
- Glasses: clean lenses (no smudges or reflections)

## PART 5: Editing Tips (Keep It Real)

You don't need Photoshop. You don't even need Facetune.

Your iPhone has everything you need.

### Use the Built-In Editor

Open your photo → Tap **Edit** → Here's what I adjust:

**Exposure:** Brighten if needed (but don't blow out the highlights)  
**Contrast:** A little boost makes the image pop  
**Saturation:** Slight increase (but not too much — you want to look human)  
**Warmth:** Adjust if the lighting was too cool/blue or too warm/yellow  
**Sharpness:** Subtle increase brings out detail (don't overdo it)

**Pro tip:** Use the "Auto" button first. Sometimes it nails it. If not, tweak from there.

### Apps I Actually Use (Optional):

**Lightroom Mobile** (free)  
- Presets + more control over shadows/highlights
- My go-to for consistent feed aesthetic

**VSCO**  
- Subtle film-style filters (I use C1 or A6)
- Great for adding a signature look

**Snapseed**  
- For selective editing (brighten just your face, darken the background)
- Healing tool removes blemishes without making you look plastic

### Editing Rules:
- **Less is more.** If you wouldn't recognize yourself, you've gone too far.
- **Consistency matters.** Pick a style and stick with it across your feed.
- **Don't over-smooth your skin.** Texture = real. Real = trustworthy.
- **Fix lighting issues with editing, but don't try to completely change your face.**

### What NOT To Do:
- ❌ Blur your skin into oblivion (looks fake)
- ❌ Over-whiten your teeth (looks creepy)
- ❌ Liquify your face shape (it's obvious and breaks trust)
- ❌ Crank saturation to 100 (you'll look orange)

## PART 6: Different Selfie Styles For Different Goals

Not all selfies serve the same purpose. Here's how to match your selfie to your goal.

### The Professional Brand Photo

**Goal:** Look polished, trustworthy, approachable.

**How:**
- Solid color top (black, white, navy)
- Clean background (plain wall, soft bokeh)
- Straight-on or slight angle
- Soft smile or confident neutral expression
- Good lighting (window light or ring light)

**Use for:** LinkedIn, website About page, email signatures, media kits.

### The Relatable Story Selfie

**Goal:** Connect with your audience. Show your real life.

**How:**
- Natural setting (kitchen, car, office)
- Candid vibe (like you just grabbed your phone to share something)
- Slight smile or mid-laugh
- Can be a bit less polished (that's the point)

**Use for:** Instagram Stories, behind-the-scenes content, personal posts.

### The Aspirational Editorial Selfie

**Goal:** Inspire. Elevate your brand. Look like a magazine cover.

**How:**
- High contrast lighting (dramatic shadows)
- Angled pose (not straight-on)
- Serious or sultry expression
- Interesting background or complete blur
- Bold color palette or monochrome

**Use for:** Instagram feed, Pinterest, brand campaigns, course covers.

### The Engagement Selfie

**Goal:** Start a conversation. Get people to comment.

**How:**
- Direct eye contact (look at the lens)
- Warm, inviting expression
- Caption poses a question or shares something vulnerable
- Feels intimate and personal

**Use for:** Posts where you want high engagement, community-building content.

## PART 7: The 10-Selfie Challenge

Want to get good at selfies fast? Do this.

**Take 10 selfies right now. All in the same spot. Change ONE thing each time.**

1. Straight-on angle
2. High angle
3. Low angle (just to see how bad it is)
4. Head tilted left
5. Head tilted right
6. Looking at camera
7. Looking past camera
8. Soft smile
9. Serious face
10. Mid-laugh (fake it till you feel it)

Review all 10. Pick your favorite 3. Notice what they have in common.

That's your formula.

Do this once a week for a month. You'll become an expert at your own face.

## PART 10: Common Selfie Mistakes (And How To Fix Them)

Let's troubleshoot the most common issues.

### "My face looks washed out"

**Problem:** Not enough contrast or color.

**Fix:**
- Increase contrast slightly in editing
- Add a touch more saturation
- Use warmer lighting (or adjust warmth in post)
- Wear colors that complement your skin tone

### "I have a double chin in every photo"

**Problem:** Camera angle is too low, or chin position is wrong.

**Fix:**
- Hold camera higher (above eye level)
- Push your chin forward and slightly down (feels weird, looks great)
- Don't look down at the screen — look at the lens

### "My skin looks terrible"

**Problem:** Harsh lighting or too much smoothing.

**Fix:**
- Use softer, diffused light (window light or ring light with a diffuser)
- Don't over-edit (texture is normal and human)
- Powder shiny areas before shooting
- Make sure your camera lens is clean

### "My eyes look dead"

**Problem:** Not enough light reaching your eyes, or you're not engaged.

**Fix:**
- Light source should be at or above eye level
- Think of something that makes you smile while shooting
- Look directly at the lens (not the screen)
- Take 20 shots — you'll loosen up after the first 10

### "My background is distracting"

**Problem:** Too much clutter or competing colors.

**Fix:**
- Choose a plain wall or simple background
- Use Portrait Mode to blur the background
- Step away from the wall (creates natural blur)
- Clean up your space before shooting

### "I look stiff and awkward"

**Problem:** You're too aware of the camera.

**Fix:**
- Take 20+ shots (you'll relax after the first 10)
- Move between shots (shift weight, turn slightly, adjust hair)
- Pretend you're FaceTiming a friend
- Play music that makes you feel confident
